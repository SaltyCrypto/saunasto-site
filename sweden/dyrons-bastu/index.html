About this venue

Dyrön is a car-free island in the Bohuslän archipelago, reached by ferry from Tjörn. The island sits between Marstrand to the north and the open Hake fjord to the south, which is to say it catches weather from two directions and has the kind of maritime light that people come to the west coast specifically for. The sauna is at the southernmost point of the island — the furthest point from the mainland, the point with the widest water view — and it sits ten metres from the sea.

The access logic matters here. You arrive by ferry. There are no cars. The Dyröleden trail runs the length of the island, and the sauna is the endpoint of that walk. You heat the sauna yourself with the wood provided, you decide when it is ready, and you finish in the sea from the jetty. The infrastructure is minimal: twelve person capacity, a communal changing area, a relaxation room. The facilities are accessible to wheelchair users, which is unusual for a remote island installation. Bookings open up to a year in advance, and the most-coveted slots fill quickly.

Rankings in this category are subjective, but the designation of best west coast sauna in Sweden by Sauna Ice in 2025 reflects something that repeat visitors already knew: the combination of isolation, maritime setting, and traditional wood-fired practice is not replicated elsewhere on this coastline at the same quality. Ribersborgs and Varberg Kallbadhus are the better-known pier institutions. Dyröns Bastu is what you find when you have already been to those and want something further from a car park.
